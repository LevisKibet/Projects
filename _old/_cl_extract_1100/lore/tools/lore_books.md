# Ancient Albion Lore Books

These entries are written as found books, scraps, ledgers, and letters for an Arthurian / Vortigern-era Minecraft lore mod. Placements are included so each text can be tied directly to structures, loot tables, lecterns, chests, or ruins.

## Village & Cottage Life

### Book 1: *Maeve's Journal, October 4th*

**Placement:** Ruined Village Houses / Cottages

**Page 1**

> King Arthur passed through today. I nearly missed him at first, because he looked nothing like the songs say. No crown. No cloak stitched with gold. Just dull mail, road mud, and a horse so tired it stood with its head low.

**Page 2**

> He stopped outside Father's forge and asked for water from our well. Father forgot to bow until Mother hissed at him from the doorway. The King laughed and said a thirsty man has no use for ceremony.

**Page 3**

> Grandfather spoke to him about the apple crop, which was poor again, and the King listened as if apples mattered as much as armies. Before he rode on, he gave Toby a silver penny and told him to keep it safe until spring. Toby slept with it in his fist.

**Placement:** Central Chapels / Sanctuary Lecterns

**Page 1**

> *From the writings of Brother Bran*  
> In the first years of Arthur's reign, he ordered small chapels built along the old roads. They were plain places: a hearth, a bell, clean straw, and a locked store of grain for winter travelers.

**Page 2**

**Page 3**

## Merlin's Own

### Book 28: *Merlin's Journal*

**Placement:** Merlin's Cabin (the lectern at his side, upstairs) only

**Page 1**

> *Written by a hand that shakes more than it did*  
> I have kept no journal in four hundred years. Memory was enough. It is not enough now. The wound in my side takes a little more of me each night, and I would leave something behind that is not a legend.

**Page 2**

> A hundred winters ago Arthur met Vortigern on the high meadow of Logres, across the western sea. Steel against steel, king against usurper. I watched from the stones. I should have done more than watch.

**Page 3**

> Vortigern struck a coward's blow, and Arthur knew he was dying. If the usurper took the blade, the kingdom was lost. So he did the only thing left to him.

**Page 4**

> Years before, I had carved a binding ward into the pedestal on that meadow - a precaution, I told myself. Arthur drove Excalibur down into it with the last of his strength. The ward took the sword, and the king's life with it, and froze Vortigern to stone beside him, still reaching.

**Page 5**

> Vortigern's warbands came up the meadow and found their chief turned to stone. They would have had me next. I ran. The old gate on the shore carried me across the water to this side, and I have not been strong enough to open it since.

**Page 6**

> They never went home. They wait for their chief to wake, and they know I am the only one who remembers how the ward was carved. So they hunt old men in grey cloaks along the roads. They caught me at the marsh chapel. The brothers there hid me, and I repaid them by bringing the raiders to their door.

**Page 7**

> This cabin belonged to a woodcutter who did not ask my name. He is gone now - I hope only to his daughter's farm. I keep to the upper room. The raiders turn the hall below over every few days, looking for me. They have not found the stair. Not yet.

**Page 8**

> I ring the bell when the mist is thick. A foolish hope: that someone with a good heart hears it before someone with a blade does. I can no longer heal myself. A healing draught, a golden apple - such small things, and I cannot reach them.

**Page 9**

> If you are reading this and I am still breathing, wake me. If I am not, find the gate I came through and cross the water. Climb to the meadow. Only one who carries my blessing can draw the sword - and when it leaves the stone, Vortigern wakes. Be ready.

**Page 10**

> I have been called prophet and devil, counsellor and fool. Tonight I am only tired. Forgive an old man his doubts. Logres has always been saved by ordinary people who arrived on time. Perhaps you are one. - M.

## The Court

### Book 29: *A Ship in the Fog*

**Page 1**

> *From the King's own hand*  
> Mordred did not come to supper again. I sat with his cup filled and watched the candles go down. When he was a boy he could not keep still at table. Now he is a grown man, and I find I do not know where he goes, or what he thinks while he is there.

**Page 2**

> Sir Bedivere came to me late. One of the coast guards had gone to his captain, and the captain to him, with a thing he did not like to say aloud. A ship off the western headland, at dusk, deep in the fog.

**Page 3**

> No lamps. No banner the guard knew. It did not come in and it did not turn away. It only sat there on the grey water, as if it were waiting for something. Then the fog closed, and when it lifted the sea was empty.

**Page 4**

> It may be nothing. A trader blown off course, a fisherman with no lamp oil. I told myself so twice. But the air in this hall has been thick for weeks, the way it is before a storm, and I have learned not to laugh at that feeling.

**Page 5**

> So: the watch on the coast is doubled from tonight. The beacons are to be kept dry and ready. And the men in the far outposts are to come home, every one of them, before the first frost. I would rather have them close and feel foolish than leave them out there alone.

**Page 6**

> I wish I could talk to Mordred about it. There was a time I told him everything first.

### Book 30: *The Empty Chair*

**Page 1**

> *From the King's own hand*  
> Mordred's chair was empty at council this morning. That is the third time this month. Nobody had seen him since yesterday's noon bell. His horse is in the stable. His bed was not slept in.

**Page 2**

> I went to his rooms myself. The fire was cold. His cloak was gone, but his sword was still on its hook, and he never goes anywhere without that sword. I stood in the doorway longer than a king should.

**Page 3**

> I have sent Bedivere's men to look for him, quietly. The road, the harbour, the river. I told them to bring him home safe and to ask him nothing. Whatever trouble he is in, he can tell me himself when he is warm and fed.

**Page 4**

> The last time he vanished he came back after two days with river mud to his knees and would not say where he had been. He smiled at me, and I let it go, because I was glad to see him. Perhaps I should not have.

**Page 5**

> Some of the lords have begun to say things when they think I cannot hear. I will not listen to it. He is the boy I taught to ride. If he is keeping something from me, it is because he is hurting, not because he means me harm.

**Page 6**

> It is nearly midnight. The chair is still empty. I will leave the door unbarred.

## The Guard Rooms

### Book 31: *Patrol Report: The Gorge*

**Page 1**

> *Sergeant Pell, river patrol*  
> Reporting what I saw on the evening patrol, though the captain says to keep it between us. We came down the path to the gorge as the light went. The tide was in and the water was high against the rock.

**Page 2**

> There was a man alone on the far bank. No lantern, no escort. Tall, dark cloak. When he turned his head I knew him. It was the prince. It was Mordred.

**Page 3**

> He did not see us. He walked along the foot of the cliff to where the rock hangs out over the water, and then he waded in. Up to his waist, up to his chest, and then he ducked his head under the overhang and was gone.

**Page 4**

> We waited a long time. He did not come back out. When the tide dropped a little I could see it: a gap in the rock, just above the water, black as a well. Cold air was coming out of it. It smelled of old stone and something else.

**Page 5**

> Old Cadan in our troop grew up near here. He says that in the war, Vortigern's people used the caves under the gorge, and that there is a lake in there under the mountain. He says Vortigern went down to that lake and gave it things. His own wife, Cadan says. His own children.

**Page 6**

> I don't hold with old men's stories. But I don't know what the King's own nephew wants with a hole in the rock that the usurper used, and I didn't like the look on his face when he went in. Someone should tell the King. The captain says it won't be us.
